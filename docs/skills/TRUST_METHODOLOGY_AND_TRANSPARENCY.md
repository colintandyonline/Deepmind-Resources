# Trust, Methodology and Transparency Skill

This skill defines how DeepMindResources should explain verification, source quality, confidence scoring, update methodology, and public trust principles.

Trust matters because AI information changes quickly and the market is full of hype, weak claims, outdated tutorials, and tool recommendations that may not match real user needs.

---

## Core Purpose

DeepMindResources should earn trust by showing how information is checked before it becomes a lesson, sandbox task, tool recommendation, AI update, or business training resource.

The platform should not simply say it is trustworthy.

It should show its method.

---

## Core Rule

Do not hide uncertainty.

Whenever DeepMindResources publishes AI intelligence, tool comparisons, lessons, sandbox tasks, or business recommendations, it should be clear whether the information is:

- Verified
- High confidence
- Medium confidence
- Low confidence
- Under review
- Conflicting evidence
- Deprecated or outdated

Weak claims must not be presented as settled facts.

---

## Trust Promise

DeepMindResources should communicate this idea clearly:

We do not publish AI hype as training.

We turn verified AI updates into practical lessons, sandbox tasks, and tool intelligence.

This promise should guide public trust pages, methodology sections, update pages, comparison pages, and business training material.

---

## Methodology Areas

The platform should eventually explain:

- How AI updates are discovered
- How sources are classified
- How claims are validated
- How confidence scores work
- How tool comparisons are created
- How lessons are updated
- How sandbox tasks are generated
- How business recommendations are reviewed
- How outdated content is handled
- How uncertainty is labelled

---

## Source Transparency

When useful, public content should explain source quality.

Useful source categories include:

- Official documentation
- Official release notes
- Official changelogs
- Official GitHub repositories
- Direct testing
- Trusted technical analysis
- Weak or unverified discovery signals

Public pages do not need to expose every internal review note, but important evidence and limitations should be clear.

---

## Confidence Transparency

Confidence should be based on:

- Source quality
- Recency
- Reproducibility
- Agreement between sources
- Whether the claim is official
- Whether there is conflicting evidence

Confidence labels should not be decorative.

They should affect whether something is published, held for review, or treated as a weak signal.

---

## Time-Sensitive Claim Rules

AI tools, models, APIs, pricing, availability, and platform rules can change quickly.

For time-sensitive claims, record and expose where useful:

- Date checked
- Source used
- Tool/model version where relevant
- Plan or region limitation where relevant
- Whether the claim may need review

Avoid evergreen wording for information that may change soon.

DeepMindResources should eventually include public methodology pages such as:

- How DeepMindResources verifies AI updates
- How AI tool comparisons are created
- How confidence scores work
- How lessons stay current
- How sandbox tasks are generated
- How we avoid AI slop
- How businesses should use DeepMindResources guidance

These pages support user trust, SEO, and AI-search understanding.

---

## Tool Comparison Transparency

Tool comparisons should show why a recommendation was made.

A transparent tool comparison should include:

- Use case
- Audience
- Tools compared
- Evaluation criteria
- Evidence or validation note
- Strengths and weaknesses
- Pricing or plan caveats
- Date checked where relevant
- Confidence or limitation note

Avoid shallow rankings and hidden affiliate-style logic.

---

## Business Transparency

Business-facing content needs extra transparency.

Business pages should clarify:

- Who the recommendation is for
- What business problem it addresses
- Tool and cost caveats
- Privacy or security concerns
- Staff training requirements
- Confidence level where appropriate
- Whether legal, compliance, or IT review may be needed

Avoid unrealistic productivity promises.

---

## Honest Uncertainty Language

Use honest language when evidence is incomplete.

Good examples:

- Official documentation currently says...
- This appears to apply to...
- Early evidence suggests...
- We have not yet verified...
- This is under review...
- Evidence is mixed...
- This recommendation may change if pricing or capabilities change...

Avoid overconfident wording when evidence is weak.

---

## Anti-Hype Rules

DeepMindResources should avoid:

- Universal tool claims
- Unsupported productivity promises
- Affiliate-style rankings
- Viral claims with no source
- Old tutorials presented as current
- Benchmark claims without context
- Repeating marketing copy as truth

Trust comes from restraint as much as publication.

## Agent Responsibilities

### Scout Agent

Records where information came from.

### Validator Agent

Checks claims and assigns confidence.

### Noise Filter Agent

Downgrades hype and weak sources.

### Tool Agent

Maintains evidence-aware tool records.

### Comparison Agent

Explains criteria and trade-offs.

### Lesson Builder Agent

Keeps lessons grounded in verified information.

### Sandbox Builder Agent

Creates transparent tasks with success criteria.

### Business Training Agent

Adds risk, cost, privacy, and staff-readiness transparency.

---

## Definition of Done

A trust-sensitive public item is complete when it has:

- Clear source basis
- Clear confidence or validation status where relevant
- Date checked for time-sensitive claims
- Practical meaning
- Caveats or limitations where needed
- No unsupported hype
- Clear separation between fact, interpretation, and recommendation

---

## Locked Rule

DeepMindResources should earn trust by showing its method.

The platform should be slower and clearer rather than fast and noisy.
