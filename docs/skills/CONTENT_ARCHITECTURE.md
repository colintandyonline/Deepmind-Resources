# Content Architecture Skill

This skill defines how DeepMindResources should organise pages, lessons, learning paths, tool intelligence, AI updates, sandbox tasks, business training content, and internal links.

Content architecture prevents the platform from becoming a messy collection of disconnected pages.

---

## Core Purpose

DeepMindResources is a living AI training and tool-intelligence platform.

Its content should guide users from:

- Discovery
- Understanding
- Tool selection
- Lesson learning
- Sandbox practice
- Skill progress
- Business training
- Current AI updates

The content structure should support the product loop:

AI update → verified meaning → practical lesson → sandbox task → user skill gain

---

## Core Rule

Every public page, lesson, tool page, update page, and sandbox task must have a clear place in the platform.

Do not create orphan pages.

Do not create pages with duplicate search intent.

Do not create content without knowing which user journey, skill, or business outcome it supports.

---

## Main Content Pillars

DeepMindResources content should be organised around these pillars:

1. Academy
2. Agentic Sandbox
3. Live AI Intelligence
4. Tool Intelligence and Comparison
5. Master Brain / verified knowledge layer
6. Business AI Training
7. Progress and skill tracking

Each major page or feature should support at least one pillar.

---

## Academy Architecture

Academy content should be organised into:

- Learning paths
- Modules
- Lessons
- Practice tasks
- Assessments where useful

Core learning areas include:

- AI Fundamentals
- Prompt Engineering
- AI Tool Selection
- AI for Business Productivity
- Tool Calling and Functions
- AI Agents
- RAG and Knowledge Systems
- Agentic Workflows
- Multi-Agent Systems
- AI Safety, Validation, and Hallucination Control

Lessons should connect to sandbox tasks whenever practical.

---

## Sandbox Architecture

The Agentic Sandbox should be organised by practical task type.

Useful categories include:

- Prompt testing
- Prompt improvement
- Model comparison
- Tool calling
- Agent building
- Agent debugging
- RAG and knowledge retrieval
- Workflow automation
- Business automation simulation
- Hallucination and validation checks
- Multi-agent simulations

Each sandbox task should connect to a skill, lesson, difficulty level, use case, and success criterion.

---

## Live AI Intelligence Architecture

Live AI Intelligence should not become a generic AI news feed.

Updates should be organised by:

- Update category
- Tool/model/platform affected
- Skill area affected
- Audience affected
- Validation status
- Lesson action
- Sandbox action
- Tool comparison action
- Business training action

Each update should point to practical next actions where relevant.

---

## Tool Intelligence Architecture

Tool pages and comparison pages should be organised by use case.

Core use-case groups include:

- Coding
- Research
- Writing
- Business automation
- Customer support
- Data analysis
- Building agents
- Internal company knowledge
- Non-technical staff workflows
- Education and training

Tool comparison pages must be scenario-based, not generic rankings.

---

## Business Training Architecture

Business training content should be organised by:

- Business role
- Team type
- Workflow use case
- Skill level
- Tool category
- Risk level
- Training pathway

Business content should connect to lessons, sandbox tasks, tool comparisons, and progress tracking where useful.

---

## Master Brain Content Architecture

The Master Brain is the structured knowledge layer behind the platform.

Master Brain records should connect to:

- Source records
- Verified claims
- AI updates
- Tool records
- Lessons
- Sandbox tasks
- Tool comparisons
- Business training notes

Raw internal Master Brain records should not automatically become public content.

---

---

## Homepage Role

The homepage should explain:

- What DeepMindResources is
- Who it is for
- Why normal AI training goes stale
- How verified AI updates become lessons and sandbox tasks
- What the Academy offers
- What the Agentic Sandbox offers
- What Live AI Intelligence offers
- What Tool Intelligence offers
- Why businesses should care

The homepage should route users into the right pillar, not contain every detail.

---

## Internal Linking Rules

Internal links should guide users from understanding to action.

Good link patterns:

- Homepage → Academy overview
- Academy overview → learning paths
- Learning path → lessons
- Lesson → sandbox task
- Lesson → related tool comparison
- AI update → affected lesson
- AI update → sandbox task
- Tool page → comparison page
- Tool comparison → lesson and sandbox task
- Business page → business learning path

Avoid orphan content with no inbound or outbound links.

---

## Search Intent Mapping

Every public content page should map to one clear search intent.

Examples:

- Learn prompt engineering
- Compare AI tools for coding
- Understand RAG
- Practise tool calling
- Train staff on AI
- Choose an AI automation tool
- Understand AI agent workflows
- Track verified AI updates

Do not create multiple pages competing for the same intent unless they serve clearly different audiences or depths.

---

## Audience and Difficulty Mapping

Content should identify its intended audience and difficulty.

Common audiences:

- Beginners
- Intermediate AI users
- Advanced builders
- Developers
- Non-technical staff
- Business owners
- Team leaders
- Training managers

Common levels:

- Beginner
- Intermediate
- Advanced
- Business awareness
- Business practical user
- Business power user
- Technical builder
- Manager/leader

Audience and difficulty should affect examples, assumptions, language, and task complexity.

---

## Avoiding Content Sprawl

Before creating new content, ask:

1. Does this support a core pillar?
2. Does this duplicate an existing page?
3. What search intent does it serve?
4. Which audience is it for?
5. Which skill does it teach or support?
6. Does it link to related lessons or tasks?
7. Does it need source validation?
8. Does it belong in the Master Brain first?

If the answers are unclear, do not create the page yet.

---

## URL and Slug Rules

URLs should be readable and stable.

Examples:

- /academy
- /academy/ai-fundamentals
- /academy/prompt-engineering
- /sandbox
- /sandbox/model-comparison
- /tools
- /tools/chatgpt
- /compare/chatgpt-vs-claude-writing
- /business-ai-training
- /live-ai-intelligence

Avoid random IDs or vague slugs for public content.

---

## Content Lifecycle Rules

Content should have lifecycle states.

Suggested states:

- Draft
- In review
- Needs validation
- Verified
- Published
- Needs update
- Deprecated
- Archived

AI content changes quickly, so lifecycle status matters.

---

## Public vs Internal Content

Not all content should be public.

Internal content may include:

- Unverified signals
- Agent findings
- Raw source summaries
- Validation notes
- Draft recommendations
- Conflicting evidence

Public content should be verified, useful, and clearly written.

---

## Definition of Done

Content architecture is healthy when:

- Every page belongs to a pillar
- Every lesson belongs to a path or skill area
- Every sandbox task practises a defined skill
- Every tool comparison has a clear use case
- Every AI update has validation status
- Public pages have clear search intent
- Internal links guide users forward
- No major content is orphaned
- Duplicate intent is avoided

---

## Locked Rule

DeepMindResources should grow like a structured intelligence platform, not like a random blog.

Every content item needs a purpose, a place, and a next action.
